03 Period & Group

Period and group, in one diagram

The periodic table organises elements by the structure of their atoms. Two organising ideas:

Period is the row. The period number tells you the highest principal quantum number (n) occupied by electrons in the atom. Sodium (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹) is in Period 3 because its outermost electron sits in the n = 3 shell.

Group is the column. Elements in the same group have similar valence electron configurations, which is why they show similar chemistry. All Group 1 elements end in ns¹. All Group 17 elements end in ns² np⁵.

The shortcut

Period = highest n in the configuration. Group = pattern in the valence shell. Get both right and you've placed the element.

04 Blocks

Blocks: where the last electron lands

The block is named after the subshell where the atom's last electron is added in the Aufbau sequence:

Each block is named for the subshell receiving the last electron

s-block elements (Groups 1 and 2) end in ns¹ or ns². p-block elements (Groups 13–18) end somewhere in np¹ to np⁶. d-block (the transition metals, Groups 3–12) end in (n−1)d¹ to (n−1)d¹⁰. f-block elements end in the (n−2)f subshell.

05 Worked Example

Locate iron (Z = 26)

Example · 3 steps

Find the period, group, and block of iron (Z = 26).

01
Write the configuration.
Fe (Z = 26): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶
Condensed form: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶
02
Read the period.
The highest principal quantum number is n = 4 (from 4s²). So Fe is in Period 4.
03
Read the block & group.
The last electron landed in 3d → Fe is in the d-block.
For d-block: group number = (electrons in (n−1)d) + (electrons in ns) = 6 + 2 = Group 8.

Iron sits in Period 4, Group 8, d-block — exactly where you'd find it on any periodic table. The configuration didn't tell you what iron does — but it told you exactly where it lives, and that location is what predicts its chemistry.

07 Summary

What you should walk away with

An element's position in the periodic table is not arbitrary — it is a direct consequence of its electron configuration. Given any configuration, you can now find:

  • PERIOD= highest principal quantum number n
  • BLOCK= subshell where the last electron lands (s, p, d, or f)
  • GROUP= number of valence electrons (with d-block special rule: ns + (n−1)d)
How this shows up in PSPM

The standard exam move is to give you four elements with proton numbers and ask you to complete a table of configuration, group, period, and block. Every concept from this unit is directly testable. Set 1 of your Topic 3 assignment opens with exactly this question.
